Key changes and impact

  • Nested questions are split onto separate pages. Accommodation, employment, debt, drugs, mental health and domestic abuse no longer use nested radios. The journey is easier to complete and meets GOV.UK accessibility guidance.
  • Alcohol and drug use are shorter. Frequency, units, why they use, and impact questions are removed. Practitioners spend less time on detail that community SAN does not need.
  • Help and wanting to make changes sit on their own pages. People only see the questions that match their answers, and the person being assessed must answer the changes question.
  • Domestic abuse is asked on separate pages. Perpetrator, victim, who was involved, and strangulation or suffocation are no longer nested. The order is clearer and safer.
  • Children and victims use the add-to-a-list pattern. Only the name or who they are is shown, with change and remove, then add another. The summary still shows the full cards.
  • Risk of sexual harm comes after the thinking questions. Yes opens those questions; no goes to the summary. People who do not need them never see them.
  • Practitioner analysis is skipped when there is nothing to analyse. Never used drugs, never drunk alcohol, and no further finance assessment skip analysis.

Insights

  • The biggest accessibility gain is splitting nested radios, not adding new questions.
  • Community SAN is shorter than the private beta: history, why, and impact questions were cut first.
  • Add-to-a-list is now the shared pattern for people, used for both children and victims.
  • Timing language is ‘the last 6 months’, not ‘before custody’.
  • Changes questions are consistent: their own page, with the hint ‘Alex must answer this question’.
  • Iteration 3 is mostly polish on relationships, children, victims and sexual harm, after the bigger Iteration 2 cuts.
